Square footage barely moves a Renaissance villa's price

This is the finding that surprises villa owners most, and it is the reason pricing a villa off dollars per square foot goes wrong here.

Renaissance villas run from 1,432 to 1,687 square feet — a spread of just 255 feet. Over the past year, 23 of them resold between $299,900 and $450,000, and the sizes did not sort the prices. A 1,432-square-foot villa sold for $377,000. A 1,687-square-foot villa — 255 feet larger — sold for $300,000. The bigger home sold for $77,000 less.

Look at it per square foot and the effect inverts: the smallest villas ran about $250 per foot, the largest about $199. That is not the market rewarding small homes. It is the market pricing villas as units rather than by footage, and letting condition, view, lot position and timing decide the rest. Within a 255-foot band, those factors matter more than the floor plan.

Practical version: if a site quotes you a per-square-foot value for a Renaissance villa, treat it with suspicion. The 23 recorded sales cluster between $300,000 and $377,000 almost regardless of size.

Not one villa in Renaissance has a pool

Community pool statistics get quoted a lot, and in Renaissance the community figure is meaningless for nearly half the homes. County records show zero of the 278 villas have a private pool. Among single-family homes, 157 of 404 do — 39 percent.

Single-family resales with a pool ran a median of $632,500 at $272 per square foot, against $490,000 and $235 without. That gap reflects lot and size as well as the pool itself, but it is real. For a villa owner, none of it applies.

Renaissance, in plain terms

Renaissance is a gated, resort-style community in Wellen Park (Venice), built by Mattamy Homes — roughly 680 to 700 homes made up of one-story paired villas and single-family homes (up to about 3,300 square feet), in Coastal, West Indies, and Spanish styles. The community is now sold out. The last builder closing recorded in county records was October 2025, so every sale on this page is an owner-to-owner resale. It is not age-restricted.

What makes Renaissance different: full amenities without bundled golf

Renaissance's identity is the affordable, full-amenity Wellen Park community without bundled golf. Residents get resort amenities — clubhouse, resort-style pool, fitness center, tennis, pickleball, bocce, dog park, and a full-time activities director — with no golf course or bundled golf cost baked into the dues. It's smaller and more intimate than some larger Wellen Park neighborhoods, and you can walk, bike, or take a golf cart to Downtown Wellen. It's often cited as one of the more attainably-priced full-amenity options in Wellen Park — a genuinely different value equation than the golf communities nearby.

Why one Renaissance home is worth more than another

Value here turns on specifics. The biggest swing factors: villa vs. single-family (separate micro-markets); preserve or water view vs. an interior lot (many homes here have water views, which command a premium); pool vs. non-pool; how close the home sits to the amenity center; outdoor-living upgrades; and larger Signature Series homes vs. smaller villas. Because the community was built in a narrow window, lot, view, upgrades, and floor plan usually matter more than a home's age. A national estimate can't see any of this; a local comparative market analysis prices it in.

Home types behave like separate micro-markets

Renaissance pairs one-story villas — popular with downsizers and seasonal owners — with single-family homes up to roughly 3,300 square feet, including larger Signature Series plans. These draw different buyers and price differently, so a villa sale isn't a reliable comp for a single-family home.

Fees and location

Renaissance is gated, with an HOA plus a CDD assessment on the annual tax bill — standard for Wellen Park communities, and something to factor into ownership cost (amounts change, so verify current figures; how gated-community fees work). It sits in the hub of Wellen Park, a short walk, bike, or golf-cart ride to Downtown Wellen's restaurants, lakefront, and events and the Marketplace shopping center, with CoolToday Park nearby and the Gulf beaches about 15 to 20 minutes away. Because the community is sold out, every sale is a resale (more on new vs. resale).

Renaissance vs. other Venice and Wellen Park communities

Buyers weighing Renaissance are usually also comparing nearby communities. Where Sarasota National is built around bundled golf and Grand Palm around nature and preserves, Renaissance is the full-amenity, no-golf, more-affordable option — and it's more walkable to Downtown Wellen than most. If you're also weighing IslandWalk or the newer Sunstone, Renaissance stands out for pairing resort amenities with a lower overall cost of ownership.
