Sarasota National, in plain terms

Sarasota National is a gated, resort-style golf community in Venice, part of the Wellen Park master-planned area just south of US-41 (Tamiami Trail). It spans about 2,400 acres — more than 1,300 of them preserve — and is certified as an Audubon International Signature Sanctuary, wrapped around an 18-hole championship course designed by Gordon Lewis. Homes were built roughly 2013 to 2020 — developed by WCI Communities, a builder known for amenity-rich, architecturally distinct master-planned communities, and later by Lennar, which acquired WCI in 2017. The community is essentially built out today, so nearly every sale is a resale (more on new vs. resale). It is not age-restricted.

The one thing that most moves value here: Golf Village vs. Lake Village

Sarasota National is split into two sections, and which one a home sits in changes both its cost of ownership and its buyer pool. Golf Village homes include bundled golf membership (two per household) in their HOA dues, which run higher as a result. Lake Village homes do not include membership — owners can still play on a pay-per-round basis and use the community amenities — and carry lower dues. Two otherwise-similar homes can be worth noticeably different amounts depending on their village, and a national estimate can't tell which is which. A local read can. Put simply, golf membership at Sarasota National is bundled with certain homes rather than offered as an option — an ownership-cost and value distinction that is unusual for the Venice area.

Home types and what drives value

The community mixes single-family homes, villas, coach and carriage homes, and townhomes, roughly 1,375 to 3,100+ square feet. Beyond village, value turns on home type, size, and view — lake, preserve, and golf-course frontage each carry different premiums. In practice, coach homes and single-family homes here often behave like separate micro-markets: they draw different buyers and their prices can move somewhat independently. And because large preserve and wetland areas are woven through the community, preserve and water views carry a more meaningful premium here than they would without that natural backdrop.

Gated, maintenance-free, and the fees to know

Sarasota National is fully gated with a 24-hour guard and is largely maintenance-free (see how gated-community fees work) — the HOA handles basic yard care and common areas. HOA dues are billed quarterly and vary by home type and village (Golf Village runs higher because golf is bundled in). There is also a CDD assessment collected through the annual property-tax bill. These amounts are set each year and change, so confirm the current HOA and CDD figures for a specific home before you buy or list.

Location

Inside Wellen Park, residents are minutes from the Downtown Wellen town center and CoolToday Park (Atlanta Braves spring training), about six miles from the Gulf beaches, and roughly seven from historic downtown Venice, with quick access to US-41 and I-75.

Sarasota National vs. other Wellen Park communities

Buyers weighing Sarasota National are usually comparing it to nearby communities, and the differences are real. It's the area's bundled-golf option — a member-centric championship course and resort clubhouse — a different proposition from a lagoon-and-town-center community like IslandWalk or a gated resort community like Gran Paradiso. Against newer Wellen Park neighbors such as Sunstone and Renaissance, Sarasota National stands out for its WCI heritage, deeper amenity package, and Audubon-certified preserve setting. The real comparison isn't just price per square foot — it's what each community includes and how that fits the way you'll live.

Why one Sarasota National home is worth more than the one next door

Inside one community, two similar-looking homes can sell for very different numbers. In Sarasota National the biggest swing factors are whether the home carries bundled (deeded) golf membership; the view — preserve, water, or golf frontage each command a premium; coach home vs. single-family, which draw different buyers and price differently; the level of upgrades and condition (with such a narrow build era, what's been done to a home matters more than its age); and lot location — privacy, cul-de-sac, and orientation. A national, Zestimate-style estimate can't see any of these and averages right over them.
