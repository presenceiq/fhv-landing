The short version

What it does, and what it does not

The relief is real. The homestead exemption on non-school taxes goes from $51,411 today to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, with inflation adjustments after that. On a typical homesteaded parcel in these communities that is a reduction of roughly $1,068 to $1,671 a year by 2028, and for some it takes the non-school line to nothing.

There is a second provision that gets less attention. The annual cap on assessment increases for non-homestead property drops from 10% to 5%. That applies to second homes, rentals and commercial property, and it makes what those owners pay more predictable.

It is not your whole bill. The increased exemption applies only to the non-school portion of your ad valorem tax. Your school tax keeps the same $25,000 exemption it has today, and non-ad valorem assessments are not affected at all: CDD, fire, solid waste, stormwater.

How much reaches zero varies enormously by community. The figure repeated statewide is that roughly 60% of homesteaded owners would owe no non-school property tax. Across the eleven communities broken out below it runs between 6% and 44%, because the exemption is a fixed dollar amount and assessed values here are high. Type any address in Sarasota, Charlotte or Manatee County above and you get the answer for that parcel rather than an average.

This page takes no position on how anyone should vote. It exists because the figures in circulation are statewide and the answer in any particular community is different. Both campaigns are making their case elsewhere: Vote Yes on 3, funded by Florida Realtors, and Florida TaxWatch and the Florida Association of Special Districts among those raising concerns.

The other side of it

Where the money comes from afterwards

Everything above is about what a homeowner stops paying. The same amount stops arriving somewhere, and that is worth saying plainly because the ballot language did not.

The amendment guarantees nothing about service funding. The Property Appraiser FAQs say so directly. No minimum is protected for law enforcement, fire, EMS, roads or anything else. Those stay where they have always been: decided every year, by each taxing authority, in its own budget.

A judge said the same thing in stronger terms. On 3 August 2026 a Leon County circuit judge ordered the ballot title and summary rewritten, finding them misleading. He specifically rejected the phrase about ensuring funding for core services, on the grounds that the amendment does not guarantee any funding level. The state chose not to appeal and the measure stays on the November ballot. What voters read changed; what the amendment does did not.

And the amendment does not limit what else a local government can charge. Non-ad valorem assessments, the ones on the bottom half of your tax bill, are set separately and are not affected by any exemption. What happens to them after 2027 is a budget question and nobody can answer it yet, including me.

The state has put numbers on the scale of it. The Revenue Estimating Conference, in the House's final bill analysis, estimated the reduction in local non-school property tax revenue at $4.95 billion in fiscal year 2027-28, $8.78 billion the following year, and $11.86 billion the year after. Those are the state's own figures, not an opponent's.

And there is a provision on the other side that gets little attention. The amendment also requires counties and municipalities to spend property tax revenue solely on a listed set of purposes: public safety, education and schools, infrastructure, natural resources, bond debt service and retirement benefits. That is a restriction on what the remaining money can be spent on. It is not a guarantee of how much there will be, and the two are easy to confuse in either direction.

How this was built

Methodology, and where it is weak

Source. The Sarasota County Property Appraiser's parcel and sales database download, 2026 certified roll, obtained 11 September 2026 from the county's own download data page. Millage comes from the same office's published 2025 rate table by tax district.

How communities were defined. By subdivision code on the county roll, not by ZIP code and not by mailing address. A ZIP grouping would be wrong here: Gran Paradiso, IslandWalk, Renaissance, Sunstone and Brightmore all carry Venice mailing addresses but sit inside the City of North Port for tax purposes and pay its municipal levy. Sarasota National carries a Venice address, is marketed as part of Wellen Park, and is unincorporated. Grouping any of these by ZIP would attribute the wrong millage to thousands of parcels.

What the model does. For each homesteaded parcel it adds back the current homestead exemption, applies the new exemption to non-school millage, and floors the result at zero. Veteran, senior, widow and disability exemptions are left intact and continue to apply to both school and non-school levies. School taxable value is assessed value less $25,000 less any of those personal exemptions, which is the structure the amendment leaves in place.

What it was checked against. Six 2026 TRIM notices, one per community across four tax districts, pulled from the Property Appraiser's own site. Non-school taxable value, school taxable value and total millage matched exactly on every one. Recomputing one notice using the rates printed on that notice produced a figure one cent from the county's own total.

Where it is weak. Four things, stated plainly.

The 2026 millage rates are not final. Budget hearings run through September, and the figures here use the county's published 2025 rates. Expect a difference of a few dollars either way.

Non-ad valorem assessments are not modelled. CDD, fire, solid waste and stormwater appear on your bill and are unaffected by the amendment, but they are not in the county's parcel download and are not calculated here. Six 2026 TRIM notices were pulled by hand to put real figures against that, one per community, and the spread is wide. A Gran Paradiso parcel carries a West Villages CDD line of $1,949 a year. An IslandWalk parcel in the same district carries $563. Palmero and Talon Preserve have no CDD at all. Those figures appear against each community in the results, labelled as single parcels rather than averages.

The calculator covers all 542,376 residential parcels in Sarasota, Charlotte and Manatee counties. The eleven communities in the table above are broken out separately because they are the ones people here ask about, and they skew newer and higher assessed than the county as a whole. Communities with older housing stock show a higher share reaching zero.

And it assumes the amendment passes as written. It needs 60% approval, a Leon County judge ordered the ballot summary rewritten on 3 August 2026 for being misleading, and implementing legislation would still follow.

Dates

What happens when

3 November 2026. The vote. It needs 60% approval, not a simple majority.

1 January 2027. If it passes, the $150,000 exemption takes effect. It first appears on the August 2027 TRIM notice and the bill mailed in November 2027.

1 January 2028. The exemption rises to $250,000, with inflation adjustments from 2029.

And a date most people have not heard about. Anyone who is a permanent Florida resident by 31 December 2026 is eligible for the larger exemption from the start. Anyone establishing Florida residency on or after 1 January 2027 begins at $50,000 and waits four years. On a home here that difference is several thousand dollars a year.
