How accurate is this?

It's a starting point, not a final answer. Automated estimates work from public records and broad market data, so they don't know about your kitchen remodel, your new roof, your view, or the fact that the house two doors down backs onto a highway. Treat the number as a range, not a price tag.

For homes in Sarasota County, Florida, I publish tools built on actual recorded county sale prices, calibrated at the subdivision level rather than the ZIP code. Those are sharper than any national estimate, because they're built from what buyers actually paid at closing in that specific community.

What to do with the number

If you're just curious, that's what this is for. If you're seriously considering selling, an automated estimate is worth about as much as a weather app is worth to a pilot: useful, and not what you'd fly on. A local agent pulling actual comparable sales in your neighborhood will land far closer, and it usually costs nothing to ask.
